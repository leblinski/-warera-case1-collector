Case I Calculator Stress Test

On the Sort and Cases tabs the calculator tells the truth about what it says it measures: the case edge is real, small, and positive on every one of 241 snapshots since 31 August, and no cheaper estimator beats the median. It is wrong in five places a user acts on, four of them on the Price tab and in the collector rather than in the six claims the brief asked about.

The six claims

1 · Case EV about +4.3%holds, level uncertain

+3.61% on this snapshot; the brief's gross 3.7114 is the 2 Sep 09:54 snapshot, whose committed ask was 3.585 (+3.53%). Sign robust; level spans +2.0% to +4.2% under defensible choices the page makes silently.

2 · Every roll equal weightholds

Removing "as traded" was right: it was +9.47%, 71% of the gap from one knife roll. Sale counts are flat only where every roll gets listed, which is what uniform draws look like.

3 · Bar as a thresholdcoherent as code, not as rationale

Monotone in the bar (3.6848 → 3.6781 → 3.3010). Not monotone in the scrap bid: 0.214 → 0.215 drops the gross by 0.027. The comment calls it a cost; a cost model gives +2.5%.

4 · The median is the priceholds

Out of sample no estimator beats the 48h median by more than 0.1 point of next-day error. The split test reproduces: +0.7 to 1.1% for 45 min versus 6 to 10 min.

5 · Neighbour fill 97.2%reproduces, weaker than it reads

97.1% on 979 rolls against a 77.5% majority base rate; forward 94.9% vs 96.6% for the roll's own price. The "never the price" rule is already broken for 253 quiet rolls.

6 · Thin marker at 5wrong, harmless

Count < 5 flags 30% of rolls at 8% precision against a 5.9% base rate; 50 of 458 flags come from the 24h window winning. A 48h IQR/median > 5% flag is 2.8× more precise.

What changes a number a user acts on

Ranked by how far the figure on screen is from the truth, times how often someone meets it. Each carries its verification status and what to measure after a fix.

wrong number · Price tabR

1. The Trends engine prices the best Common roll at 1.859 with HIGH confidence; it clears at 4.0 to 4.33

Common knife 40/5, the one Common roll worth listing: the roll card says "List at 4.329" and the Trends panel beneath it says "Historical target 1.859 · HIGH 73". Type a floor of 1.800 and the page upgrades that to "History confidently supports 1.859 above the current 1.800 floor", a higher-price opportunity 57% below the clearing price. The mechanism is weaponComparables (line 5834): it takes twelve nearest-score comparables, and for 40/5 eight of them are broken 37–39 rolls at 1.85–2.40, so robustFilter's ±12% band around their median throws out all four genuine 40/5 sales. Three of 301 weapon rolls are more than 10% off this way (sniper 130/17 by −17%); armour is unaffected because singleModel uses exact rows when three exist.

Fix: in weaponModel, when three or more exact-roll sales exist use robustFilter(exact) alone, as singleModel does; never let lower-attack rolls of a Common or Uncommon weapon in as comparables for the band-maximum attack. Measure: max |hist − exact q55| over band-max weapon rolls should fall from 53.5% to under 2%; knife 40/5 at a 1.80 floor must report a target within 5% of 4.329.

wrong number · Price tabR

2. The Trends "minimum undercut" ties the rival's price on 1% of floors, 4.8% at 5% tax

The core docket undercuts correctly on every one of 49,999 floors from 0.002 to 50.000. The Trends panel does not: it sets the target to floor − 0.001 and converts it with the non-beat conversion (showFinal, line 6317), which rounds up whenever floor − 0.001 is a displayed value no typed figure can produce. At 1% tax that is 495 floors; a floor of 1.869 yields "type 1.850", which displays at 1.869, a tie, where the docket path gives 1.849. The two headline figures on the same screen disagree on those floors.

Fix: in showFinal and showDismantleInstead, when the target is the minimum undercut, use entryForDisplayed(state.floor, true); after any non-beat conversion check that the typed figure displays at or below the target and step down 0.001 if not. Measure: verify/undercut.js ties must be 0 at every tax.

wrong verdict · Sort, Craft, CasesR

3. A missing scrap bid silently makes every roll worth listing

craftDismantle returns 0 when commodities.json has no scraps bid or no order book, and nothing downstream distinguishes "no bid" from "scraps are worthless". Served that file, the Sort tab reads "scraps fetch 0.00 · list above 0.10", 180 of 180 seen rolls "sell" (151 at baseline), every Uncommon slot flips from "none" to "every roll", and the Cases tab prints +2.72% with Break 0% on every tier. The Price tab, which reads its bid from index.json, still shows a 1.337 floor beside all this. The same page also quotes three floors for one Common piece at baseline: 1.337 (Price tab, ÷1.01), 1.35 (roll card and Sort), 1.465 (cut card, bar included).

Fix: return null from craftDismantle when there is no bid and have every caller show the "—" and message the Craft tab already shows; read one scrap source everywhere. Measure: re-serve case_d_scraps_nobid: the Cases verdict must be "—" or carry a warning, the Sort header must not print 0.00, and #scrapVerdict must equal the Sort tab's "scraps fetch".

wrong number · all tabsR

4. Three tax models on one page

The data settles one thing: money is on the seller's typed 3-decimal grid. If it were the tax-inflated displayed price, round3(typed × 1.01) could never produce values on the residue 50 + 101k, yet 900 of 126,800 sales sit exactly there (1.868, 2.474, 2.777). So the transaction feed records what the seller typed. The Price tab's flip maths treats that as what the seller receives (buyer pays typed × 1.01); the Sort, Craft and Cases tabs net median × 0.99 instead; paintScrap divides the scrap floor by 1.01. These cannot all be right. If the seller nets the typed figure the case edge is +4.16% not +3.61%, Epic crafting is +2.6% not +1.6%, and 39 of 1,040 Sort verdicts flip from break to sell. 123 priced rolls sit between the Price-tab floor and the Sort floor: one tab says list, the other says break, for the same piece.

Fix: decide once whether the seller nets the typed figure, route every tab through one netOfSale and one netOfScrap, and say which model the card assumes. Measure: the Price-tab dismantle floor and the Sort "list above" must agree to the grid; the between-floor count must be 0.

lost data · collectorR

5. The backstop scan loses sales after any outage longer than three hours, and a fresh cache can never bootstrap

In full-scan mode collect_market stops as soon as a page is older than now − 3h (line 371), before it checks whether the checkpoint has been reached. After the 229-minute gap on 1 Sep the next run stopped at 3 hours and the checkpoint jumped forward: the rolling cache holds 41, 0, 0, 0, 6 sales in the five 10-minute buckets from 01:50 to 02:40 against 75–150 on either side. The same happened on 2 Sep 04:30 (bucket of 10 between 93 and 61). About 454 sales are gone from the cache and the archive, and every outage over 3 hours will repeat it. The 6-hourly backstop that caused this has recovered nothing in 240 run pairs: only 4 sales ever arrived later than the previous run, all inside the 0.5h overlap.

Separately, history_complete was set once, on the first run at 08:42 on 31 Aug when retention was 48 hours, and has been inherited on every run since; the retained window has never been paged to 168 hours (oldest sale 29 Aug 00:31 against a cutoff of 27 Aug 15:30). The stream costs 4 to 19 pages per hour of history, not the README's 62, so a week is 1,800 to 2,700 pages against max_pages 1000 and a 1,200-request time budget. Delete the cache, or switch --base-url, and every run pages to 1000, errors, keeps last_success_at at None, fails --require-healthy, and repeats.

Fix: make the backstop stop also require oldest < overlap_cutoff; on a never-complete cache stop at an initial depth sized to the page budget (about 48h), mark complete, and let retention accumulate forward, recording the depth reached instead of a boolean. Measure: after any inter-run gap over 3h, sales per 10-minute bucket across the gap must not collapse; a scratch run from an empty cache must reach status ok in one run.

optimistic · Sort, roll cardR

6. "Sells in" is the median of sales that cleared inside two days, and "8 minutes" matches no population on the page

The collector drops sales off listings older than 48 hours before it takes the time-to-sell median, so the figure is censored: 311 rolls dropped at least one sale and every one of them has a higher true median (ratio 1.19); on 60 rolls, 28 of them Legendary, the true median is more than double what is shown. The brief's 8 minutes is the split test's at-or-below-median population (7.2 minutes on the brief's own snapshot), not a figure any page cell shows, and not what a seller meets: the median over the rolls a case tells you to list is 84 minutes (Basic 103, Legendary 132, Mythic 265), and sales priced above the roll median take four times longer than those at or below it (42 vs 10 minutes overall). The Uncommon tier's 1.3-minute median is bot sniping at scrap value, not liquidity: the top ten buyers take 48% of Uncommon volume, the top one 2,538 pieces at a median 4.000 in 0.4 minutes.

The survivorship half of the doubt is real but bounded: among sales at or above the roll median, 4.5 to 8.6% sat over 48 hours in the sellable tiers (7.5 to 14.6% over 24 hours). Valuing that share at scrap instead takes the edge to +2.97% (48h share) or +2.60% (24h share); those are the values of a break-at-48h policy rather than corrected EVs, since the sales that define the share did clear at or above the median and their real cost is delay (about +3.4% at 1% carry a day). The edge reaches zero only if 34% of median-priced listings never sell.

Fix: compute median_time_to_sell_seconds over every eligible sale in the window and publish the dropped count; label the cell "median of sales that cleared within 2 days". Measure: published versus uncensored tier medians should differ by ~0 after the change (now 0.12h Basic, 0.23h Legendary, 0.56h Mythic).

optimistic · Cases, CraftR

7. Unpriced rolls are assumed to price like the priced ones, and the knife is 46% priced

craftSlotValue averages only rolls with a selected median. The knife is 30% of Common and Common is 62% of cases, so 18.6% of every case outcome rests on 46 priced rolls, and the 54 unpriced ones are not a random sample: they sit at crit 2 to 4, attack 21 to 38, while the priced mean is pulled up by 40/5 at 4.33. Valuing the missing rolls the way the Sort tab's own fill would, week median where one exists and the nearest worse priced neighbour otherwise, gives a knife of 1.831 instead of 1.898 and a case of 3.6653 (+3.25%); at scrap, 3.6211 (+2.00%). Tank and jet move nothing; their odds are too small.

Fix: give an unpriced roll the same neighbour or week-median value fillQuiet already finds, and print the filled share in the foot. Measure: knife slot value ~1.83 and case gross ~3.665 on this snapshot; the foot should say 98 of 100 filled, not 46 of 100 priced.

wrong number · Price tab with API keyR

8. Half of live sales double-count against the shard

The shard stores each sale's second as int(timestamp), a floor; saleKey (line 5672) keys live rows with Math.round(ms/1000). 63,184 of 126,800 sales have milliseconds ≥ 500, so every Gateway page re-adds about 50 of its 100 rows as "+N live", and they are the newest rows, which carry the most recency weight. Only users with an API key see it.

Fix: Math.floor in saleKey. Measure: a live page fully inside the shard's window must add 0 rows.

wrong verdict · Price tabR

9. A typed 4.329 opens the Uncommon Gun grid for a Common knife

Common and Uncommon overlap from 2.835 to 4.385 and "higher tier wins". 94% of Common knife 40/5 sales sit inside the Uncommon range, so the one Common roll worth pricing lands on the Uncommon rarity, the Gun slot, an attack grid of 51–60, and a 4.010 dismantle floor, unless the user notices the "also legal for Common" note. The RARITY_BANDS fallback is stale too: it puts 8.0 in Uncommon when nothing Uncommon traded above 4.445 and 92.0 in Epic when Epic tops out at 79.5.

Fix: when a price hits both tiers and the slot is the weapon, prefer Common inside the knife 40/5 range or ask; regenerate the fallback bands from observed ranges. Measure: Common sales assigned to Uncommon by price, now 5.8%, should fall to the irreducible overlap.

stale data unflagged · data stripI

10. The data strip tracks one file while the numbers come from two others

paintDataStamp reads index.json's generated_at; the figures come from summary.json and commodities.json, and collectorFile keeps the old copy when a refresh fails. A refresh where the index updates but the other two 404 prints "Prices collected 1 min ago" over 25-minute-old figures with no failure text. When the index alone fails, the strip says "Collector prices not loaded yet" while every tab quotes full figures and the Price tab silently falls to a hard-coded 0.218 scrap price. The page never reads schema_version: rename selected and it quietly prices every roll from the 48h window instead.

Fix: stamp the oldest of the three files actually used, say when a refresh partially failed, and refuse a schema other than 4. Measure: the harness cases case_q_stale_refresh, case_a_index404 and case_i_schema5_chosen must each show a warning instead of a number.

inconsistent verdicts · SortC

11. Smaller inconsistencies that show one roll two verdicts

  • The weapon heat grid colours a cell by margin over scrap (line 5337) while the list uses the bar: gun 60/10 and rifle 80/14, 87/11, 89/11 are painted green and read "break" when tapped.
  • The "Per 1,000" column counts only priced rolls: Common sums to 520 of 620, and the knife row says 86 when a thousand cases hand out 186 knives.
  • The swing line's "12,100 cases before profit beats the swing" uses the price of one case; 12,100 cases walk the book to 3.590, and the self-consistent figure is about 28,900. The simulator's "Expected" line multiplies the one-case edge by its own n and reads 5% high at 1,000 cases, 42% at 9,999.
  • "sales / hour" on the Price tab is a recency-weighted count over a ±5-stat band, 1.3 to 7.9 times the exact roll's rate for armour and capped at twelve comparables for weapons; it drives the queue-hours and the SAFE / LESS SAFE / RISKY bands.
  • Quick/Patient mode has no control in the HTML and nextCursor is always null, so the 0.70 quantile, the 6-hour queue limit and the "widen to Nh" scan are dead code.
  • A negative tax is accepted: at −5% the Price tab applies it while the other tabs clamp to 0 under a chip that says "Tax −5%".
  • For quiet rolls with a week median the Sort list prints that median as "List at", contradicting the stated rule; the 32 low knife rolls that get a dash instead receive 5.95% of case draws.

The claims in detail

Claim 1 · the case edge

The page's arithmetic is exact. A Python re-implementation of craftSlotValue, craftExpected, paintCase and craftWalk reproduces the rendered card to the fourth decimal (gross 3.6781, +3.61%, tier rows 1.58 / 4.06 / 13.47 / 50.81 / 137.77 / 379.83, break 13 / 99 / 64 / 0 / 3 / 9%), and re-deriving every selected median from the shards matched all 1,040. The brief's gross of 3.7114 is the snapshot committed at 09:54 on 2 Sep (a1664b87, 1,060 priced rolls); its committed case ask was 3.585, giving +3.53%, so the 3.558 in the brief was presumably the live Gateway book the page walks when an API key is set, and the +4.3% is that day's gross against a better ask than the collector saw.

What the level rests on, in order of weight: the Uncommon tier is 1.2167 of the 3.678 gross and is 99% broken, so a third of the case is 18 scraps at the 0.225 bid (654,018 scraps rest there; the walk moves nothing even at 10,000 cases, but each 0.001 of bid is 0.0065 of gross); the knife coverage above; the tax model; and the price definition. Bracketing the last from the shards: p25 of the 48h window +2.70%, p75 +4.46%, last sale +3.58%, median of sales that cleared within 30 minutes +3.34%, 48h minimum −2.37%. Stacking the two most defensible corrections (knife fill, survivorship at the 48h share) gives about +2.6%; under the seller-nets-typed tax model about +3.2%. So +4% is the top of the plausible range and +2 to +3.5% its centre. The sign never flips: the edge reaches zero only if every median overstates next-day clearing by 9.3%.

The sanity argument ("3,500 cases within 1% of the ask, nobody takes free money") cannot separate +4% from +9%. The binding constraint is not case depth but Common listing volume: with 0.325 Common listings per case an opener is 10% of Common sales at about 390 cases a day and 50% at 1,940, the edge at the 10% cap is about 50 gold a day, and beating the 7.06-per-case swing at two standard errors takes 12,100 cases, 43,000 gold of capital and a month at that rate. That is labour, not arbitrage, at either figure. The data cannot check throughput either way: the collector discards case1 transactions, so daily case volume is unknown.

Claim 2 · equal weight per roll

Removing the weighting was right and the evidence is stronger than the mechanism argument alone. Sale counts per roll are flat across the band exactly where the Sort tab says every roll sells (Epic boots 1754 / 1816 / 1613 / 1569 / 1693; sniper attack 499–609 per value; tank, Legendary armour flat) and skewed to the top roll exactly where low rolls are told to be broken (knife attack 40 is 600 of 1,712 sales; Basic boots 161 / 162 / 186 / 288 / 728; helmet3 130 rising to 477). The "as traded" figure of +9.47% is 71% knife 40/5, which carried 29% of the knife's weight on 2.2% of its draws. One residual: the Epic sniper's band maximum sells 15% more often than uniform (3.5σ on attack, 5.3σ on crit) with flat Epic armour beside it, unstable day to day (×1.02 to ×1.33). Applied to the knife it would move the gross by +0.003. Only a case-opening log that records the roll before any listing decision can settle whether the RNG favours the band edge; sales never will.

Claim 3 · the bar

As a description of the code the claim is exact: the bar gates, it is never subtracted, the EV is monotone in it, and an absurd bar lands on 3.3010 = 0.225 × Σ odds × yield. Two things are less tidy. The comment at line 4406 justifies the bar as the attention a listing costs, and a cost is paid on the listings that clear the bar too. The comment's "five copper" is the forgone edge per dropped listing, which only implies a cost of at least 0.05; charging 0.05 on the 0.379 listings per case gives 3.6591 (+3.07%), charging 0.10 gives 3.6402 (+2.54%), and the bar of 0.10 is exactly the optimal threshold for a 0.10 fee, so the page reports the fee-free row of a fee model. And the threshold makes the EV discontinuous in the scrap bid: at bar 0.10 a bid rise from 0.214 to 0.215 pushes the Uncommon need across the 4.00 price cluster, break goes from 9% to 93%, and the gross falls 3.6411 → 3.6137; nine such down-steps exist in a 0.001 sweep, none under max(sale − 0.10, scrap). That is the nature of a threshold rather than a bug; the fix, if one is wanted, is to show a second figure net of a user-set listing cost.

Claim 4 · the median

Holds. Rolling-origin test over 8,927 roll-origins and 245,238 scored next-day sales: plain 48h median 2.89% relative error; the collector's selected window 2.84%; its recency-weighted median 2.82% (the only statistically real gain, 0.06 points); trimmed mean, last-3, p25, p75, recency mean and plain mean all worse. The switch to a 24h window at three sales is at the minimum of a 1-to-20 sweep. The split test reproduces at a threshold of seven non-stale sales in 48h: 558 rolls, 43,844 sales, Q3 earns +0.72% by roll median (+1.05% mean) and clears in 45 minutes against 6 to 10; Basic is the exception where Q3 buys +3.6% for nine times the wait. The one caveat the brief should carry: the test conditions on the sale having happened, so it shows the median clears fastest, not that it earns the most; the observable proxy is that 61.6% of next-day sales clear at or above the median estimate against 47.9% for Q3.

Claim 5 · neighbour fill

Reproduces: 97.1% held-one-out over the 979 priced rolls that have a lower neighbour (61 sit at the bottom of their band and get none), knife 100%, rifle 94.7%, confusion 749 / 25 / 3 / 202. But always saying "sell" scores 77.5%, and a per-item majority rule 91.5%; the neighbour beats the base rate by more than ten points at Rare (92% vs 58%), Basic (96% vs 82%) and Mythic (96% vs 85%), and matches it elsewhere. Scored forward against the next 24 hours it is 94.9% against 96.6% for the roll's own price, a gap of 1.2 points once the scrap bid of the day is used. On the rolls it actually serves, quiet with no week median, it is 100% on 173 cases, of which 172 are true sells: its break side has never fired in the wild. The "lower bound two thirds of the time" is the strict figure (70%; 79% with ties). The "never the price" half is not what the page does: 253 quiet rolls get the 7-day median printed as "List at", and that price is 86% within 5% of the next day's median, about as good as a marked neighbour floor (75% within 5%, biased low), so the dash on the knife's low rolls is the costlier choice.

Claim 6 · the thin marker

Wrong on both counts, harmless because no verdict or EV depends on it. Subsampling 386 rolls with 30 or more sales, the p90 error of a small-sample median is 5.5% at n=1, 6.2% at n=2, 2.7% at n=3, 2.2% at n=5, 1.4% at n=10: the knee is at three, nothing singles out five, and a Basic roll with 30 sales (p90 13.7%) is less certain than an Elite roll with two (1.5%). Out of sample the count-under-5 flag marks 30% of roll-origins at 8% precision for a next-day miss over 5%, against a 5.9% base rate; only n=1 stands out (7.2% p90). A 48h IQR/median over 5% flags 10% at 22% precision, though half of that advantage is the Basic tier, where prices are simply dispersed; outside Basic the IQR flag and a count-of-one flag are at parity, and "tier is Basic" alone reaches 32% precision. The count the marker reads is selected.count, so 50 rolls with five or more sales in 48h are flagged thin because the 24h window won with three or four. The min–max range on the Price tab is outlier-driven: 62% of unmarked rolls span more than 10% of the median while only 3% have an IQR over 10%.

The collector

Beyond finding 5, the collector is sound where it claims to be: the 41 tests pass plus 21 extra ones written for this review; the tamper check, migration, atomic writes, redirect refusal and retry rules behave; ids are unique across archive days and every completed day matches the rolling cache. Three more things worth knowing.

  • The stale filter (listings over 48h old are not comparables) is doing something different from what it was built for. In steady state 1,197 of the 1,354 sales it drops were priced above the roll's median: it removes over-priced listings that eventually cleared, nudging medians down by a few hundredths of a percent, whereas the bracket move it was written for had stale sales below the market. It also removes 31 to 33% of knife and helmet1 sales, so 32 knife rolls that did sell in the week look "never traded" on the Sort tab. A price-at-listing test would do the job the comment describes.
  • Archive day files are not write-once. publish compares the whole record including schema_version, so the two schema bumps rewrote every completed day (about 26 MB of repository each) with zero row changes.
  • The cadence is documented three ways: "every 10 minutes" in the README intro, 15 in its scheduler section and the page, "10-minute cadence" in two page comments. Measured: 241 runs, median gap 14.9 minutes.

Method and reproduction

Both repositories were read end to end. A reference implementation (ev_ref.py) mirrors the page's economics; a Playwright harness (harness.js) serves test60.html over HTTP with every collector URL answered from shards regenerated locally by collector.publish and every other host answering 403 as the proxy does, so the rendered DOM was read for every figure quoted here. Eight investigators each attacked one dimension with their own scripts; every finding above marked R was then re-run independently by me with a fresh script, C means confirmed from the code, I means the investigator's harness output was spot-checked but not re-run. An adversarial refuter pass then re-ran 23 of the 46 substantive findings independently (the EV, roll-odds, estimator and neighbour-fill dimensions) before a session limit stopped it: none was refuted, five had their severity lowered to robustness or nit, and the numeric corrections it produced (the Common volume cap, the provenance of the 8-minute figure, the per-tier neighbour scores) are folded in above. The 24 findings it did not reach are the seam, collector, Price-tab and numerics ones, each of which is marked R because I re-ran it myself.
